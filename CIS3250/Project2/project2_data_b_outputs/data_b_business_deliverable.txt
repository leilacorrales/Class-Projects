DATA B: CUSTOMER TYPES AND TARGETED MARKETING RECOMMENDATIONS

K-means separates the 8,950 customers into 4 groups based on purchases, cash advances and outstanding balance. The analysis uses log-transformed, standardized values so that monetary scale and extreme observations do not dominate distances.
The features were chosen for their business relevance: purchases indicate card spending, cash advances distinguish cash-access usage, and balance describes outstanding amounts. No customer identifier, protected attribute or supervised target enters the model.
For k=4, silhouette is 0.5295 (higher is better), and Davies-Bouldin is 0.6803 (lower is better). The elbow heuristic suggests 4 clusters. Scores compare k=2 through k=10; silhouette uses a fixed random sample of 2,500 customers.

SEGMENT INTERPRETATION AND STRATEGIES
Cluster 0: Mixed purchases and cash advances
Customers combine purchase activity with cash advances and have relatively high balances. They represent a distinct mixed-use group.
Arithmetic mean purchases: 1,239.38; cash advances: 2,125.15; balance: 2,698.29.
Recommended strategy: Test retention messaging and rewards tied to existing purchase activity, alongside transparent cash-access costs and optional payment-management tools. Do not infer repayment capacity or automatically raise credit limits from this segment.
Evaluate the pilot: Track incremental purchases, retention, reward cost and payment-tool engagement.

Cluster 1: Cash-advance-focused, minimal purchases
Customers use the card primarily for cash advances, with almost no purchasing activity. Their outstanding balances are substantial relative to the low-balance group.
Arithmetic mean purchases: 0.29; cash advances: 2,049.77; balance: 2,212.96.
Recommended strategy: Offer clear cash-access cost information, optional payment reminders and budgeting tools. Test a modest purchase-activation incentive for customers who want to use the card for everyday spending. Avoid treating cash usage alone as proof of financial distress or eligibility for more borrowing.
Evaluate the pilot: Track voluntary purchase activation, offer cost, engagement with payment tools and customer retention.

Cluster 2: Low-balance purchasers
Customers make purchases while holding low observed balances and using very little cash advance. Low balances alone do not prove that they pay in full every period.
Arithmetic mean purchases: 409.80; cash advances: 11.30; balance: 39.53.
Recommended strategy: Test low-cost everyday-purchase rewards and card-engagement messages. Keep incentives modest because current purchase amounts are lower than those of the purchase-focused group.
Evaluate the pilot: Track active-card usage, incremental purchases, incentive cost and retention.

Cluster 3: Purchase-focused customers with balances
Customers emphasize purchases, with almost no cash advances, and maintain higher balances than the low-balance purchasers.
Arithmetic mean purchases: 1,998.61; cash advances: 0.26; balance: 1,322.28.
Recommended strategy: Test purchase-category rewards or merchant offers, paired with useful spending summaries and optional payment reminders. Tailor messages to purchase activity without assuming that these customers are premium, profitable or low risk.
Evaluate the pilot: Track offer redemption, incremental purchases, retention and net campaign contribution.

Run small controlled marketing pilots before rollout. Measure incremental campaign contribution after rewards and communication costs, rather than treating cluster membership as proof of profitability.
This segmentation describes observed behavior. It does not establish income, creditworthiness, repayment capacity, full-payment behavior or causal effects. Cluster labels are arbitrary, and conclusions depend on selected features and transformations. Reassess profiles with refreshed data before reusing them.
